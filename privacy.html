Privacy

What data this site touches, who it goes to, and what we don't do with it. Plain English, no scroll-trap.

Last updated · 6 May 2026
  • We don't run our own servers, accounts, or databases. There's nothing to log in to.
  • To answer "should I go outdoors?", your approximate location is sent to a small number of third-party APIs, listed below.
  • We don't sell your data, build a profile of you, or email you.
  • Once Google AdSense is live, ads on the page may use cookies. You'll see a consent banner where required, and you can refuse.

Who we are

shouldigooutdoors.com (the "site") is a single-page weather verdict tool, operated by Nine185. If you need to reach us about anything in this policy, write to info@nine185.com.

What we handle

The site touches two pieces of information about you, and only for as long as it takes to produce a verdict:

  • Your approximate location — either GPS coordinates from your browser (if you grant permission), or a city name you type in. Coordinates are rounded before being sent anywhere, so the precision is roughly neighbourhood-level, not address-level.
  • Your IP address — visible to any service your browser contacts. We don't log it on infrastructure we control beyond what our hosting provider (Cloudflare) retains for routine network operations and abuse prevention.

We don't ask for your name, email, account details, or payment information. There's no sign-up. There's no profile.

Where your data goes

Producing the verdict requires sending your location to outside services. Below is the complete list of third parties involved, what each one receives, and why.

Service What it gets Why Their policy
Open-Meteo Approximate latitude/longitude (rounded), or a city name when you type one in. Returns the rain forecast and resolves typed-in city names to coordinates. open-meteo.com/en/terms
BigDataCloud Approximate latitude/longitude (rounded). Reverse geocoding — turning your coordinates into a readable city name to display. bigdatacloud.com/privacy-and-cookie-policy
ipapi.co Your IP address. Only contacted if your browser blocks geolocation and we fall back to IP-based lookup. Estimates an approximate city when no other location is available. ipapi.co/privacy
Google AdSense Cookies, device/browser information, IP address, and ad-interaction signals, per Google's policies. Serves advertising and measures performance. Active only once AdSense is live and, where consent is required, only after you accept. policies.google.com/technologies/ads
Cloudflare Standard request metadata (IP, user agent, headers) needed to deliver the page. Hosting, content delivery, TLS, and abuse/bot mitigation. cloudflare.com/privacypolicy

We don't control how these third parties use the data they receive. Their own privacy policies, linked above, govern that.

Geolocation

The site asks your browser for an approximate location, which you can deny. If you refuse, the site will fall back to either an IP-based estimate or a manual city entry. You can revoke geolocation permission at any time through your browser's site-settings UI.

When coordinates are sent to a third-party service, they're rounded so precision is limited to roughly a city block — enough for an accurate forecast, not enough to identify a specific home.

Cookies

The site itself doesn't set tracking cookies. Any client-side storage is functional — for example, briefly remembering your last lookup so you don't have to grant location access again on reload.

Google AdSense, when active, may set cookies for ad delivery, frequency capping, and (where you've consented) personalisation. In the EEA, the UK, Switzerland, and other jurisdictions that require it, a Google-certified consent banner will appear before any ad cookies are set, and you can decline. Anywhere in the world, you can opt out of personalised advertising via Google Ads Settings.

What we don't do

The shorter list, by design.

  • Sell, rent, or share your location, IP, or anything else for marketing purposes.
  • Run cross-site analytics or fingerprinting scripts that profile you.
  • Maintain user accounts, mailing lists, or any way to contact you on our own initiative.
  • Store your location queries on infrastructure we control.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have legal rights over personal data — for example, the right to access, correct, delete, or restrict processing of data about you, the right to data portability, the right to withdraw consent, and the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection authority.

Because we don't maintain accounts or store your queries, in most cases there's nothing for us to retrieve or delete on our side. For data held by the third-party services listed above, you'll need to contact them directly using the policies linked in the table.

If you have a request that involves us specifically, write to info@nine185.com and we'll respond within a reasonable timeframe.

Children

The site is a general-audience utility and isn't directed at children. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided information through the site, please contact us and we'll take appropriate steps.

International transfers

The third-party services listed above may process data in countries other than the one you're visiting from, including the United States and the European Union. By using the site, you understand that your data may be transferred to and processed in those jurisdictions, subject to the safeguards described in those services' own policies.

Security

The site is served exclusively over HTTPS. We rely on Cloudflare for transport security, DDoS protection, and abuse mitigation. No transmission over the internet is ever 100% secure, but we apply industry-standard practices to keep your interactions with the site safe.

Changes

If we change how the site handles data — a new third-party service, a new ad format — we'll update this page and bump the "Last updated" date at the top. Material changes will also be reflected in the FAQ on the home page.
